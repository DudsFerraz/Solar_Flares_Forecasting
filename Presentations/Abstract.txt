Title: Investigating the Most Relevant Aspects for Solar Flare Forecasting Through a Hierarchical Machine Learning Architecture Introduction: In this study, we investigate the most relevant aspects for the prediction of solar flares  by evaluating two primary space weather forecasting perspectives: Cause-to-Effect (using magnetic physics) and Past-Effect-to-Future-Effect (using time series). Our focus is on evaluating the efficacy of these approaches, treating astrophysical phenomena as input variables in complex classification methods to predict the maximum flare class within future time windows.Methodology: The datasets employed in this study consist of global X-ray flux data from the GOES (NOAA) satellite  and regional magnetic data from the SHARP dataset. The data underwent a rigorous preprocessing pipeline , including feature engineering with sliding windows , dynamic acceleration rates , and intensity ratios. To address the severe class imbalance where X-class events represent only 0.06% of the data , we developed an original cascade architecture named 'Solarfall'. Instead of a single model, Solarfall utilizes four specialized Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) models —Gatekeeper, Great Filter, Specialist 910, and Specialist MX —that progressively filter the data. The training methodology incorporated strict chronological partitioning , automatic feature selection to retain 95% cumulative importance , and Bayesian hyperparameter optimization using Optuna.Results: We found that the hierarchical Solarfall architecture allows each model to train on a more specific and balanced dataset. Through comprehensive evaluation using metrics such as the True Skill Statistic (TSS), Matthews Correlation Coefficient (MCC), and PR AUC, we analyzed the models' trade-offs between precision and recall at mathematically adjusted decision thresholds. Baseline comparisons, including Persistence Relative F1 (PR-F1) and Activity Change versus No Change (AC vs NC) scenarios, revealed that while the models excel at diagnosing stable solar states, predicting abrupt changes remains challenging. Furthermore, error distribution analysis highlighted critical insights into the models' behavior across safe zones and high-danger thresholds, demonstrating the architecture's capacity for refining extreme event detection
